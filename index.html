Case File 01

Introduction to Media and Information

Lesson Summary

Literacy :

The ability to identify, understand, interpret, create, communicate, and compute using printed and written materials associated with varying contexts.

Literacy involves a continuum of learning wherein individuals are able to achieve their goals, develop their knowledge and potential, and participate fully in their community and wider society.

Information :

A broad term that covers processed data, knowledge derived from study, experience, instructions, signals, or symbols.

Media:

The physical objects used to communicate with, or the mass communication through physical objects such as radio, television, computers, film, etc.

It also refers to any physical object used to communicate messages.

Media Literacy:

The ability to access, analyze, evaluate, and create media in a variety of forms.

It aims to empower citizens by providing them with the competencies (knowledge and skills) necessary to engage with traditional media and new technologies.

Information Literacy:

The ability to recognize when information is needed, and to locate, evaluate, and effectively communicate information in its various formats.

Technology Literacy:

The ability of an individual, either working independently or with others, to responsibly, appropriately, and effectively use technological tools.

Using these tools an individual can access, manage, integrate, evaluate, create and communicate information.

Media and Information Literacy:

The essential skills and competencies that allow individuals to engage with media and other information providers effectively

Developing of critical thinking and life-long learning skills to socialize and become active citizens

Media Literacy, Information Literacy, and Technology Literacy:

Media literacy - highlights the capacity of an individual to understand the functions of media and determine the relevant use and worth of media platforms.

Information literacy - is the communication or acquiring of data or facts that relate to the use of media information.

Technology literacy - is the skill of an individual to manipulate technology independently or with the assistance of others in using the technology in an efficient and suitable way.

Communication and Media Information:

Communication is affected by media and information because of the advancement of technology and the availability of different communication services.

These allow us to have access to fast and easy forms of communication through the use of different media platforms and social media sites like Facebook, Messenger, Instagram, Twitter, among others.

Responsible Use of Media and Information:

The 21st Century is known as the information age. Now, people are highly dependent on technology in different aspects of their lives such as for communication, entertainment, and work-related activities. Some social media are agents of change.

Here are some tips for the responsible use of media and information:

Ensure personal information safety - Do not display or give away your personal information on your social media account.

Get permission - When posting videos or pictures of other people on your social media account, do not forget to ask for the owner's approval and of the people who appeared in the video or picture.

Think before you click - When posting or sharing on social media, think first if the information you are going to post or share is accurate, helpful, informative, nice, and kind to others.

Keep your password safe - Keep an eye on your account; do not let others know your password.

Spread positivity - Share positive and uplifting posts to your friends.
-----
Media and Information Literacy involves necessary skills that let a person interact using different media platforms and get access to information around the globe. These should be learned because we are now living in the information age when the media have a significant role in our society towards disseminating information, connecting people, and acting as an agent of change In our society.

Lesson Reflection

Media and information are already a big part of our everyday life, especially since we use our phone and social media almost every day. I usually scroll through posts, watch videos, chat with people, and search for information without really thinking about how much media I actually use. Because of this lesson, I understood that being media and information literate is not just about knowing how to use technology, but also knowing how to use it properly.

I can relate this to my own experience on social media because there have been times when I saw a post that looked believable and almost shared it, only to find out later that some of the information was wrong or missing context. It made me realize how easy it is to believe something just because a lot of people are sharing it. Sometimes, even the comments can make something seem more convincing. Now, I know that it is better to stop for a moment and check the source before believing or sharing anything.

The lesson also reminded me that being responsible online is more than just keeping my password safe. It also means respecting other people's privacy, asking permission before posting their pictures, and thinking about how my posts might affect others. Since social media is already a normal part of my daily life, these are things I can actually apply instead of just remembering them for a lesson.

Overall, I think Media and Information Literacy is useful because we deal with information almost everywhere. It taught me to be more careful with what I believe, post, and share online. Being active on social media is easy, but being a responsible user takes more thought.

Case File 02

The Evolution of Traditional to New Media

Lesson Summary

Media has evolved throughout history as technology developed and improved communication among people.

Pre-Industrial Age (Before 1700s)
Early people discovered fire, created tools, and developed methods of recording information such as cave paintings, clay tablets, papyrus, Acta Diurna, Dibao, and woodblock printing.

Industrial Age (1700s–1930s)
The Industrial Revolution introduced machines that allowed mass production of printed materials. Examples include printing presses, newspapers, typewriters, telephones, telegraphs, and motion pictures.

Electronic Age (1930s–1980s)
The use of electricity led to the development of radios, televisions, computers, and other electronic devices. Communication became faster and reached wider audiences.

Information Age (1900s–Present)
Also known as the Digital Age, this period is characterized by the internet, social media, smartphones, cloud computing, and advanced digital technologies that allow rapid communication and information sharing worldwide.

Summary:
The evolution of media shows how communication continuously improves through innovation. From cave paintings to social media, media has become faster, more accessible, and more interactive.

Lesson Reflection

The lesson on the Evolution of Media helped me understand how communication has changed throughout history.

Before learning this lesson, I only thought about modern technology such as smartphones, social media, and the internet. I did not realize that people in ancient times already had ways of recording and sharing information through cave paintings, clay tablets, and written documents.

I can relate this lesson to my daily life because almost everything I do involves modern media. I communicate with friends through messaging apps, search for information online, and watch videos for entertainment and learning. Knowing how media evolved made me appreciate the technologies that we often take for granted today.

The lesson also made me realize that every generation adapts to new forms of media. Just as people before learned to use telephones, radios, and television, our generation continues to adapt to digital platforms and social media. This reminds me that technology will continue to change, and I need to be open to learning new tools and ways of communicating.

Overall, the Evolution of Media showed me how important media is in connecting people and spreading information. Understanding its history helps me appreciate modern communication and encourages me to use technology responsibly.

Case File 03

Types of Media

Lesson Summary

Media can be classified according to how information is delivered and consumed.

Print Media
Uses paper and ink to communicate information. Examples include books, newspapers, magazines, journals, newsletters, and brochures.

Text Media
A simple and flexible format for conveying ideas through written words, whether handwritten or printed.

Visual Media
Refers to pictures, photographs, images, graphics, infographics, cartoons, and other visual elements used to communicate messages.

Broadcast Media
Uses airwaves to transmit information. Examples include radio and television.

Audio Media
Uses recorded sounds or voice as a medium of communication.

Multimedia
Combines text, graphics, images, video, animation, and audio in a digital format.

New Media
Refers to modern digital platforms such as blogs, wikis, podcasts, augmented reality, and video games.

Media Convergence
The merging of different media technologies into one device or platform. Smartphones are examples of media convergence because they combine camera, internet, radio, messaging, and video functions.

Summary:
Different types of media serve different purposes, but all help people create, access, and share information effectively.

Lesson Reflection

This lesson made me realize that media is present almost everywhere and comes in many forms.

Before studying this lesson, I usually associated media with social media platforms only. However, I learned that books, newspapers, radio programs, photographs, videos, and websites are all forms of media that help deliver information to people.

I use different types of media every day without noticing it. I read messages, listen to music, watch videos, browse websites, and communicate through social networking sites. Learning about the different types of media helped me understand how information is shared in different ways depending on the purpose and audience.

The concept of media convergence stood out to me because smartphones combine several forms of media in a single device. This convenience makes communication easier but also requires responsible usage.

Overall, this lesson helped me appreciate the different types of media and their role in everyday communication and learning.

Case File 04

Media and Information Sources

Lesson Summary

Reliable information comes from different sources that help people gather accurate knowledge.

Main Sources of Information

Indigenous Knowledge
Knowledge unique to a particular community and passed down through oral traditions, cultural practices, legends, myths, songs, and rituals.

Library
A place containing books, journals, databases, and other resources that provide access to information for research and learning.

Internet
A global network of interconnected computers that provides access to information and communication services worldwide.

Classifications of Information Sources

Primary Sources
Original materials such as interviews, speeches, photographs, surveys, research results, letters, and personal records.

Secondary Sources
Sources that analyze, explain, or interpret information from primary sources. Examples include reviews, commentaries, and monographs.

Tertiary Sources
Sources that organize and compile information from primary and secondary sources such as directories, bibliographies, and yearbooks.

Summary:
Understanding different sources of information helps individuals locate reliable data and evaluate information effectively.

Lesson Reflection

The lesson on Media and Information Sources taught me that information can come from many different places.

Most of the time, I immediately search the internet whenever I need information. Because of this lesson, I learned that there are also other reliable sources such as libraries, indigenous knowledge, and primary documents.

I realized that not all information online is accurate. Some websites may contain incomplete or misleading information. This is why it is important to evaluate sources carefully and compare information from multiple references.

Learning about primary, secondary, and tertiary sources helped me understand how information is organized and how research should be conducted properly. It made me more aware of the importance of using reliable evidence in school activities.

Overall, this lesson taught me to be more critical and responsible when searching for information and to always check the credibility of my sources.

Case File 05

Media and Information Languages

Lesson Summary

Media language refers to the codes, symbols, conventions, and formats used to communicate meanings through media.

Denotative Meaning
The literal and direct meaning of a media text.

Connotative Meaning
The implied meaning that is influenced by culture, beliefs, values, and experiences.

Media Codes

Symbolic Codes
Include settings, costumes, colors, props, and acting that create meaning through interpretation.

Technical Codes
Include camera angles, editing, audio, and lighting techniques used in media production.

Written Codes
Include printed text, dialogue, captions, and lyrics used in media products.

Media Conventions

Form Conventions
The expected arrangement of media content.

Story Conventions
The narrative structures, characters, and viewpoints used in storytelling.

Genre Conventions
The common themes and elements associated with a particular genre.

Summary:
Media language helps audiences understand messages by using specific codes and conventions that create meaning beyond simple words and images.

Lesson Reflection

The lesson on Media and Information Languages helped me understand that media messages are carefully designed to communicate meaning.

Before this lesson, I usually focused only on the message itself. I did not pay much attention to the colors, camera angles, sounds, and symbols used by media creators. After learning about media codes and conventions, I realized that these elements influence how audiences interpret information.

I can see this in advertisements, movies, and social media content. Different techniques are used to persuade people, create emotions, and attract attention. Knowing this allows me to become more aware of how media messages affect my thoughts and decisions.

The lesson also taught me that different people may interpret the same media message differently because of their experiences, culture, and beliefs. Understanding this helps me respect the perspectives of others.

Overall, I learned to analyze media more carefully and not simply accept messages at face value.

Case File 06

Legal, Ethical, and Societal Issues in Media and Information

Lesson Summary

Digital citizens must understand their rights and responsibilities when using media and information.

Intellectual Property (IP)
Refers to creations of the mind such as inventions, literary works, artistic works, trademarks, and designs.

Types of Intellectual Property
• Copyright
• Patent
• Trademark
• Industrial Design
• Geographical Indications and Appellations of Origin

Fair Use
Allows copyrighted works to be used for purposes such as research, teaching, reporting, criticism, and commentary under certain conditions.

Creative Commons
Provides copyright licenses that allow creators to legally share and distribute their works.

Netiquette
Refers to proper online behavior and respect toward others in digital environments.

Digital Footprint
The trail of data left behind by a person's online activities.

Digital Divide
The gap between people who have access to technology and those who do not.

Computer Addiction and Cyberbullying
Excessive use of the internet may negatively affect mental health, while cyberbullying involves using digital platforms to harm, harass, or embarrass others.

Summary:
Responsible use of media requires understanding legal, ethical, and social issues that affect digital interactions.

Lesson Reflection

The lesson on Legal, Ethical, and Societal Issues in Media and Information is very relevant to my daily life because I frequently use the internet and social media.

I learned the importance of respecting intellectual property and giving proper credit to content creators. Many people share or copy content online without realizing that it may violate copyright laws.

The discussion about netiquette also reminded me that people on the internet deserve the same respect as people in real life. Being responsible online means using respectful language, protecting privacy, and thinking before posting.

I also became more aware of my digital footprint. Every post, comment, and activity online contributes to a record that may remain available for a long time. Because of this, I should be more careful about what I share online.

Overall, this lesson helped me become more aware of my responsibilities as a digital citizen and encouraged me to use technology appropriately and ethically.

Case File 07

Opportunities, Challenges, and Power of Media and Information

Lesson Summary

Media and information provide many opportunities but also present challenges and risks.

Opportunities
• Online Shopping
• Citizen Journalism
• Online Education
• Easier Communication
• Faster Access to Information

Challenges
• Age-inappropriate content
• Illegal content
• Privacy invasion
• Identity theft
• Phishing scams

Threats and Risks
• Fake news
• Piracy
• Libel and Slander
• Cybercrime

Influence of Media

Educational
Enhances teaching, learning, and critical thinking skills.

Social
Connects people and influences relationships and interactions.

Economic
Creates jobs, promotes business opportunities, and supports online entrepreneurship.

Political
Encourages public awareness, participation, and communication between government and citizens.

Summary:
Media is a powerful tool that can educate, inform, and connect people when used responsibly.

Lesson Reflection

This lesson showed me that media and information provide many opportunities but also create challenges and risks.

I enjoy the benefits of media through online learning, communication, and entertainment. Access to information has become easier than ever, and people can learn almost anything online.

However, I also realized that media has certain dangers such as fake news, scams, identity theft, and privacy issues. These problems remind me to be careful when interacting online and to verify information before accepting it as true.

The lesson made me realize that media has a strong influence on education, society, the economy, and politics. Because of its power, media can shape opinions, behaviors, and decisions.

Overall, I learned that media is a powerful tool that can bring positive change when used responsibly and critically.

Case File 08

Current and Future Trends in Media and Information

Lesson Summary

Media and information technologies continue to evolve and shape the future of communication and learning.

Current and Future Technologies
• Haptics Technology
• Contextual Awareness
• Voice and Tone Recognition
• Intelligent Routing
• Eye Tracking Technology
• Internet Glasses
• Artificial Intelligence (AI)
• Virtual Reality (VR)
• Augmented Reality (AR)

3D Technologies
• 3D Printing
• 3D Movies
• 3D Holograms

Massive Open Online Course (MOOC)
A learning system that provides online courses accessible to large numbers of learners from around the world.

Types of MOOCs
• xMOOC – follows a traditional course structure.
• cMOOC – focuses on collaboration and learner participation.

Advantages of MOOCs
• Accessibility
• High-quality learning resources
• Flexible schedules
• Self-paced learning
• Global collaboration

Popular MOOC Platforms
• Coursera
• edX
• Udacity
• FutureLearn

Summary:
Emerging technologies and online learning platforms continue to transform how people communicate, learn, and interact with information in the modern world.

Lesson Reflection

The lesson on Current and Future Trends in Media and Information made me excited about how technology continues to evolve.

Many of the innovations discussed in the lesson, such as artificial intelligence, virtual reality, eye-tracking technology, and smart devices, seemed futuristic but are becoming more common in everyday life.

I was particularly interested in Massive Open Online Courses or MOOCs because they allow people from different parts of the world to access quality education online. This shows how technology can make learning more accessible and convenient.

The lesson also reminded me that although technology provides many benefits, it should still be used responsibly. New technologies can improve communication and learning, but they also require users to think critically and use information carefully.

Overall, this lesson taught me that the future of media and information will continue to bring exciting opportunities. To keep up with these developments, I must remain open to learning, adaptable to change, and responsible in my use of technology.
